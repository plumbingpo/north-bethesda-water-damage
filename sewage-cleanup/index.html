Warning Signs for Sewage Cleanup

Recognizing the warning signs early can save you time, money, and prevent significant damage to your property. Watch for these telltale signs that professional help is needed:

  • Every sink, tub and shower draining slowly even after you plunge them
  • Sewage residue visible around drain openings or on basement floors
  • Damp patches on a wall or floor that also smell foul
  • Unexplained illness or gastrointestinal symptoms among household members
  • Gurgling sounds from toilets or drains when other fixtures are used
  • A toilet overflow containing waste that has spread onto flooring or carpet
  • Wastewater seeping from the sewer cleanout or pooling in the yard above the line

How to Prevent Sewage Cleanup Problems

Taking preventive measures can help you avoid costly water damage issues down the road. Follow these tips from our IICRC-certified technicians:

  • Do not wait on a slow drain, which can turn into a full blockage
  • Have your sewer line inspected with a camera every 2 to 3 years to catch potential problems
  • Protect against municipal sewer backups by having a backwater valve fitted where your line connects
  • Flush nothing but toilet paper, because wipes, even 'flushable' ones, cause clogs
  • Clear tree roots near sewer lines, which can infiltrate and block the pipes
  • Never pour grease, oil or food waste down your drains, since these cause blockages

The Benefits of Professional Sewage Cleanup

This is one cleanup you should never take on yourself. The biological hazards in raw sewage require specialized training, proper personal protective equipment (Tyvek suits, respirators, protective gloves and boots) and EPA-registered disinfection products that are not available to consumers. Exposure to sewage pathogens can cause serious illness, including gastrointestinal disease, hepatitis, respiratory infections and skin infections.

Contaminated materials count as biohazard waste, and disposing of them properly is part of a professional cleanup. Putting carpet, insulation or drywall soaked with sewage out with household trash breaks the law and endangers sanitation workers and your community. Certified restoration companies maintain proper waste disposal protocols and relationships with licensed biohazard waste disposal facilities.
