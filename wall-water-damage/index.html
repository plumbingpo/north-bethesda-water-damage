Wall and Drywall Water Damage: What North Bethesda Homeowners Should Know

A sheet of drywall behaves much as a wick does. Once the base of a wall gets wet, moisture climbs through the paper and gypsum, commonly a foot or more higher than the water stood on the floor. Insulation and the rear of the panel can be wet through while the paint out front shows nothing unusual. That is why we check walls with a moisture meter instead of trusting how they look.

A wet wall does not automatically mean demolition. Drywall that got wet with clean water and is still firm can often be dried in place, sometimes by removing the baseboard and moving air through small holes into the cavity. If the drywall has gone soft, crumbles, shows mold or took in dirty water, we do a flood cut, meaning a level cut over the damaged area with all material beneath it removed.

Signs to Look For

  • Mildew smell you notice most when standing close to one section of wall
  • Flooring along the wall that is damp, stained or starting to lift
  • Fasteners and joint lines becoming visible on a once smooth wall
  • Speckled growth showing up where furniture sits against the wall or at corners
  • Drywall that feels soft, spongy or crumbles when pressed
  • Wall coverings lifting, blistering or creasing close to floor level
Behind the Surface

Risks That Are Easy to Miss

Moisture in Exterior Walls

On an exterior wall, water can soak the sheathing behind the insulation. Because it is slow to dry, we test it before any new drywall goes up.

Wet Outlets and Wiring

Receptacles and cables sit low in the wall, right where water collects. Leave the outlets on a damp wall alone until someone has checked the circuit.

Soaked Batts in the Wall

Once wet, fiberglass insulation stays wet and keeps the studs damp. No amount of airflow on the painted side will dry a cavity packed with wet batts.

Mold Inside Cavities

The back of the drywall is dark, still and lined with paper, which is ideal for mold. It can cover the hidden side while the painted face looks clean.

How We Fix It

Our Wall and Drywall Water Damage Process

1

Map the Moisture

Every wall is checked with a thermal camera and moisture meter, and we mark the height and reach of the water, covering the other face and the rooms next door.

2

Decide What Stays

Based on the water category, how long it sat and how the drywall feels, we tell you plainly which sections can be dried in place and which need to come out.

3

Open Up or Air Out

For walls being saved, baseboards come off and air is directed into the cavity. For walls being removed, we make a level flood cut and take out wet insulation.

4

Drying and Reconstruction

Drying typically takes 3 to 5 days of air movement and dehumidification before the framing and surviving drywall meter dry. Then we insulate, hang and finish new drywall, reinstall trim and paint.

If Nothing Is Done

What Happens if You Wait

The first few hours

Water Starts Climbing

Water at the base of the wall soaks into the drywall edge and starts to climb. The trim and the framing plate at floor level get wet too.

Hours 24 to 48

Swelling and Blisters

Moisture keeps climbing, the baseboards puff up and blisters form in the paint. Mold can start on the paper facing inside the cavity.

Day 3 to 7

Panels Lose Strength

Paper peels from a core that is no longer firm. Insulation slumps inside the wall, odor builds and mold spreads across the hidden back side.

Past one week

Framing Is Affected

Mold and the start of decay show up on framing that has been wet the whole time. Much more wall now has to be removed and rebuilt.
